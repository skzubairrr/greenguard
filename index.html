Report an Incident

A report with a live photo, GPS and timestamp becomes a public case on the tracker, not a message that quietly disappears.

Why GPS, time and a live photo?

Facts make a report verifiable and hard to dismiss. Photo, GPS and timestamp go together in the case record.

Privacy rules

Blur faces and number plates before upload. GreenGuard records land damage, not people.

Accountability Tracker

Every report is public. Officials can update a case status through a link — quick action shows as green. No response recorded in the app for 7 days marks a case as “Ignored”.

How It Works

Five steps from raw data to public accountability.

1

Satellite flags change

Periodic satellite comparisons flag large green-cover changes in watch zones.

2

Citizens verify

Nearby volunteers confirm on the ground with a live photo, GPS and timestamp.

3

Cameras give proof

Pilot cameras at high-risk spots provide continuous recorded evidence.

4

Report goes to Forest Department

The complete case is submitted to the relevant authority for action.

5

Public tracker shows the result

Status stays visible on the tracker: acknowledged, acted on — or ignored.

⚠️ Honest limits

  • Satellites miss small cuts — individual trees are below detection and verification needs people or cameras.
  • Cameras are a small pilot — 3 sample sites cannot cover every trail or road.
  • GreenGuard cannot force authorities to act — it only makes ignoring a report visible.
  • “Ignored” means no response recorded in the app — the department may have acted without updating the status.
  • No claim of illegality is made without official or court confirmation — this app publishes facts and asks questions.
